# West African point interpretation

For each point, read the marked location on the whole image and its matching native-detail view. Return the class of the land unit at the centre. The displayed second acquisition, when present, is aligned to the same point. Compare structure on both dates and use the label period to interpret any visible clearing, canopy growth or construction.

Use 0 for cropland or other open land, 1 for other woody cover, scrub, wetlands or water, 2 for Cashew orchards, 3 for built-up land, and 10 for unresolved cover. Give one short observation from the marked unit. The same month can have different local conditions across West Africa.

- **Cashew, class 2.** On a clear view, prioritize broad, blunt-rounded crown outlines that repeat within the marked land unit. Follow the recurring soft, rounded lobes across a mature canopy where individual outlines merge. In a sparse or irregular orchard, inspect several individual rounded crowns and trace the gaps through the point. Warm or bright green can accompany this texture. A continuous cashew canopy can occupy wetter landscapes or border a river. Rows, uniform colour and grove size vary. At an edge, inspect the crown or ground exactly at the mark before assigning the surrounding patch's class.
- **Other woody cover, class 1.** Follow the trees inside the marked patch across the whole view. A repeated mix of strongly different canopy layers and crowns, a coherent tidal or drainage pattern, a recognisable alternative crop pattern, or naturally distributed shrubs in a continuous scrub matrix can support this class. Nearby water, uneven spacing, a dark tone and a closed canopy also occur in orchards. Where a coherent-looking orchard shares these cues with woodland, inspect the local patch for a distinguishing feature. Fine, similarly spaced crowns may occur in other tree crops. Treat real water surfaces and characteristic wetland patterns as class 1.
- **Cropland and open land, class 0.** Trace the cultivated ground, narrow crop lines, exposed soil or fallow through the point. In sparse tree cover, compare the continuity of the field or open area and any separate patch with regularly recurring orchard-sized crowns.
- **Built-up, class 3.** Look for identifiable structures, precise roof corners and cast shadows or a yard connected to a compound. A bare patch without visible built morphology can belong to a different land unit.

Assign 10 when the ground at the point is obscured, the date evidence leaves a plausible change unresolved, or the locally observed woody cues still fit both Cashew and other woody cover. Country names and a familiar landscape help orient the view. The actual land unit supplies the evidence for a definite decision.
